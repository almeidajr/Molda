# Fabric preparation and folding references

Checked 2026-10-15 for issue #43. This is reference verification, not a physical
trial or confirmation of garment fit.

- [Seamwork: Cutting and marking fabric](https://www.seamwork.com/sewing-tutorials/cutting-and-marking-fabric)
  supports preparing and pressing fabric, identifying grain and selvages, and
  avoiding distorted alignment. Directional prints and nap require layout care;
  this guide does not choose an actual layout for those fabrics.
- [Heather Handmade: Easy Circle Skirt, page 4](https://www.heatherhandmade.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Easy-Circle-Skirt-Tutorial-Free-PatternC-1.pdf)
  describes a one-piece layout folded lengthwise then crosswise, marking from
  the corner with two folds, conditional on fabric being wide enough. Only this
  folding geometry is used; its knit/elastic sizing and construction are not
  Molda's waistband/closure assumptions.
- [Mood: DIY Not? How to Make a Circle Skirt, Full Circle section](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/diynot-how-to-make-a-circle-skirt/)
  independently identifies the corner where both folds meet, with no raw edges,
  as the radius origin. Molda retains its own validated radii and allowances.

## Diagram orientation

The left-to-right then top-to-bottom directions are our geometric orientation
of the referenced method, not a quotation from a source. Start with selvages
left/right and cut ends top/bottom. After folding the left selvage onto the
right, the closed fold is left and aligned selvages are right. After folding
the top raw end onto the bottom, closed folds meet at the upper-left corner
of the retained bottom-right quarter; raw ends are below and selvages right.
The marking artboard uniformly enlarges this quarter without rotating it.

The cloth illustration assumes enough fabric. It does not measure actual fabric,
check fit, account for directional layouts, or provide a complete garment
pattern. Start with paper or scrap; instructions for the opening, closure
allowances and waistband are required before cutting garment fabric.

## Circular cutting and unfolding (#44)

Checked 2026-10-15 against the same sources. Heather Handmade's page 4 specifies
cutting the marked corner arc first, then the hem arc through the folded layout.
Mood's full-circle section independently supports cutting along the two curved
marks from the closed-fold corner. These support the physical order only: Molda
uses its own calculated cutting radii, including seam and hem allowances, rather
than either source's elastic/knit dimensions or construction assumptions.

Keep the four layers aligned, cut the waist cutting arc then the hem cutting arc,
and remove the central and outer scraps. Do not cut the straight folds. The
stitching and finished-hem arcs remain references; a zero allowance makes the
corresponding reference and cut coincide.

Unfolding reverses our established folds: open the upper pair upward around the
horizontal fold to form a right semicircle, then open the upper layer left around
the vertical fold. The four quarter-annuli make a complete annulus with a central
waist cutout. This reversal is the geometric consequence of the referenced folds,
not a claim that a participant has tested this guide. The opening animation
uniformly reduces the quarter to half its marking-screen size to fit the full
circle, retaining the same origin and all radius ratios.

No straight closure slit is demonstrated. The full circle is not a complete
pattern and does not guarantee fit. Separate opening, closure-allowance and
waistband instructions are needed before cutting garment fabric. The displayed
sequence remains for paper/scrap practice pending the #45 participant trial.
